Response: We thank the reviewer for this observation. We agree that the small back-and-forth movements visible after some saccades in Video S1 are an interesting feature of the P. vitticeps recordings. To quantify their occurrence, we identified large saccades that were followed within 100 ms by a second saccade directed approximately opposite to the initial movement (circular direction difference > 90°; latency ≤ 100 ms from primary onset). Such rapid post-saccadic reversals occurred following 41.5% of large saccades (n = 1834/4416 events).

We next asked whether these events were associated with head movement. Post-saccadic reversals occurred in 48.0% of large saccades during head-moving periods (n = 1438/2997) compared with 27.9% during head-stationary periods (n = 396/1419; two-sided Fisher’s exact test, odds ratio = 2.38, P < 0.001). Thus, these back-and-forth movements were strongly associated with concurrent head movement. We interpret this association as consistent with nystagmus-like compensatory eye movements contributing to gaze stabilization during head motion, rather than the eye necessarily remaining fixed at its initial post-saccadic position as is commonly observed following primate saccades. We have added this analysis to Fig. Sx and discuss this interpretation in the revised manuscript.
